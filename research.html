In review

Making use of pre-aggregated life cycle impact assessment results: Methods and examples

Edwards, P., Myers, RJ.

Abstract

Increasing the availability of high-quality inventory data is an ongoing challenge in life cycle assessment, for example due to time and resources required for primary data collection, and confidentiality limitations on operating data. Pre-aggregated life cycle impact assessment (LCIA) results for commercial products are increasingly published by manufacturers in the form of an ‘EPD’ (Environmental Product Declaration), which report LCIA results and are subject to data quality requirements such as data age, collection methods, and mandatory validation under EN 15804, making them a potentially rich source of product data to fill inventory data gaps. We demonstrate two methods for using pre-aggregated LCIA results in new process-based calculations and propose a third method to calculate the life cycle inventory (LCI) from pre-aggregated LCIA results. This work can be used to support simplification of the inventory data generation procedure, improve the utility of pre-aggregated LCIA results in EPDs, and increase transparency of reported LCIA results.

In review

Performance of alternative Portland clinker feedstocks and substitutes: A multi-criteria evaluation

Edwards, P., Leal da Silva, WR., Fennell, P., & Myers, RJ.

Abstract

Portland cements emit 7-8% of anthropogenic CO2, mostly due to limestone calcination during Portland clinker production. This study evaluates eleven materials which could reduce CO2 emissions (either by clinker substitution, or displacing limestone in clinker production) based on a qualitative rubric assessing six factors: cost (2024 USD); global availability; environmental impact; health and safety; and technical performance as clinker substitute and clinker feed. The best clinker substitutes are calcined clay and natural pozzolans, due to their wide global availability and low production costs (<$30 tonne—1). High performance materials for clinker feed are CaO-rich blast furnace slag, which we estimate could reduce calcination emissions by ~16%. We recommend materials that perform well in both applications should be prioritised in clinker feed. We find the local context is important to material suitability. Results of this work informs the priority data development to improve accuracy of environmental assessments of potentially lower impact cements.
